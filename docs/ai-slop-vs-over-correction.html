AI slop vs over-correction

One is timid padding. One is performed edginess. They look opposite, they read the same — and most humanizers cure the first by causing the second.

When people ask why a humanizer "made it worse," this is almost always the reason. The tool took text that was guilty of AI slop — hedging, filler, dead transitions — and corrected so hard it landed in the opposite ditch: over-correction, the forced-hot-take, em-dash-heavy, "let's be honest" voice. Both are slop. They share the same root cause: a paragraph with no real claim, dressed up to look like it has one.

Subtractive editing fixes the first without producing the second. The point isn't to make writing punchier or softer — it's to find whether a claim exists, state it plainly if it does, and flag the paragraph if it doesn't.

The same hollow paragraph, both costumes

Here is one empty idea wearing each outfit, then said plainly:

As slop: "It's worth noting that, in today's fast-paced environment, choosing the right tool can often play a key role in driving meaningful outcomes for your team."
As over-correction: "Your tool isn't the problem. Your process is. Most teams won't admit it — but the best ones already know."
Said plainly: — and here the paragraph simply ends, because it never had a claim. Neither costume can hide that. The honest move is to flag it and ask the writer what they actually meant.

This is why "flag, don't fabricate" matters. A detector that only knew AI-slop tells would bless the over-corrected version. A real humanizer has to catch both — and admit when there's nothing underneath.

How to tell which one you have

  • Count the qualifiers. If sentences open with "it's worth noting" or stack two hedges, you have slop. Subtract them.
  • Count the reveals. If sentences keep setting up a false belief to knock down — "you've been told X; here's the truth" — you have over-correction. Remove the performance.
  • Apply the deletion test. Cut the sentence. If nothing is lost either way, the paragraph is hollow — not a candidate for rewording in either direction.

FAQ

Why did my AI humanizer make the writing worse?
It over-corrected. Tools that apply rules like "be punchy, drop em-dashes, go second-person" trade timid AI-slop for a louder, equally recognizable voice. The writing feels different but reads just as artificial, because the underlying paragraph still has no real claim.
Are em-dashes a sign of AI writing?
Dense em-dashes manufacturing emphasis are a tell, and over-correction leans on them heavily. But banning the dash outright is itself over-correction — a single earned dash is fine. Density and intent matter more than the character.
What's the difference between AI slop and over-correction?
AI slop says too little too softly: hedging, filler, dead transitions. Over-correction performs conviction it never earned: forced hot takes, negative listing, staccato fragments. They're opposite surfaces over the same hollow center.
Can one tool catch both?
It has to, or it isn't honest. A detector tuned only for timid slop will pass the edgy kind, and a humanizer that only softens or only sharpens just moves text between the two ditches. The fix is to flag both shapes and, when a paragraph has no claim, say so.
